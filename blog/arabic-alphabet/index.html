Arabic Alphabet: All 28 Letters with Names, Sounds and Forms

All 28 letters in one chart, the sounds English speakers find hard, how letters join and a simple plan to learn them in weeks.

The Arabic alphabet has 28 letters, written from right to left. Every word of the Quran is built from them, so learning the letters well is the first step to reading the Quran. This guide gives you all 28 letters with their names and sounds, explains the letters English speakers find hardest, shows how letters change shape when they join and offers a simple plan for children and adults.

Key takeaways
  • Arabic has 28 letters, written and read right to left.
  • Most letters have up to four forms: on their own, at the start, middle and end of a word.
  • Short vowels are written as small marks called harakat: fatha, kasra and damma.
  • Several letters have no English equivalent, so they must be learned by ear from a teacher.

The 28 Letters of the Arabic Alphabet

Here are all the letters in their standard order, with their names and the closest English sound. Where there is no English sound, the note explains how it is made.

What about hamza?

The hamza (ء) is a glottal stop, like the break in "uh-oh". It sits on an alif, waw or ya, or on its own. Many Qaida books teach it alongside alif.

Why the order matters

The Noorani Qaida teaches the letters in this standard order, then groups similar shapes together. You can follow along with our free Noorani Qaida PDF and Noorani Qaida guide.

Letters English Speakers Find Hardest

Most Arabic letters are easy for English speakers. A handful need real practice with a teacher.

Throat letters

ع, ح, غ, خ, ء and ه all come from the throat. The ع and ح are the hardest, because English never uses the middle of the throat. Our guide to makharij al-huroof explains exactly where each one comes from.

Heavy letters

ص, ض, ط, ظ, ق, غ and خ are "heavy" (full-mouthed). The back of the tongue rises and the sound becomes deeper. Mixing heavy and light pairs is the most common beginner mistake:

س and ص

Seen is light, Saad is heavy.

ت and ط

Ta is light, the heavy Ta is deep.

د and ض

Dal is light, Daad is heavy and unique to Arabic.

ذ and ظ

Both "th as in this", but Dha is heavy.

ك and ق

Kaf is from the front, Qaf from the very back.

ه and ح

Soft h from the chest, sharp h from the throat.

Why it matters in the Quran

Swapping a heavy letter for a light one can change the meaning of a word. This is one reason Tajweed starts with the letters themselves.

How Arabic Letters Join

Arabic is written in a joined script, like English handwriting. Most letters connect to the letters on both sides and change shape slightly.

Letter Alone Start Middle End
Ba ب بـ ـبـ ـب
Jeem ج جـ ـجـ ـج
Ayn ع عـ ـعـ ـع
Ha (soft) ه هـ ـهـ ـه

The six letters that never join forward

ا, د, ذ, ر, ز and و join to the letter before them but never to the letter after. That is why you see small gaps inside words such as دَار.

Dots make the difference

Many letters share a shape and differ only by dots: ب ت ث, ج ح خ, د ذ, ر ز, س ش. Children often learn these as "families".

Harakat: The Short Vowels

Letters alone are consonants. Small marks above or below them, called harakat, tell you which vowel to say.

The three main harakat

Fatha ( َ ) above the letter gives an "a" sound, kasra ( ِ ) below gives "i", and damma ( ُ ) above gives "u". So بَ is "ba", بِ is "bi" and بُ is "bu".

Sukoon, shaddah and tanween

Sukoon ( ْ ) means no vowel. Shaddah ( ّ ) doubles the letter. Tanween ( ً ٍ ٌ ) adds an "n" sound at the end of a word. These are taught in the later lessons of the Qaida, together with noon sakinah and tanween rules.

A Simple Plan to Learn the Arabic Alphabet

Most children learn the letters in 4 to 8 weeks with short daily practice. Adults often take 2 to 4 weeks.

Week 1: letters 1 to 7, by sound and shape. Say each one aloud ten times.

Week 2: letters 8 to 14, then mix them with week 1.

Week 3: letters 15 to 21, with extra time on heavy letters.

Week 4: letters 22 to 28, then the full alphabet daily.

Weeks 5 to 8: joining forms and harakat, reading two and three letter words.

Tips for children

Use flashcards, trace letters in sand or on a tablet, and sing the letters in order. Keep sessions to ten minutes. Our online Quran classes for kids and Noorani Qaida online classes make the letters playful.

Tips for adults

Focus on sound first, shape second. Record yourself and compare with your teacher. If you are starting from zero, our online Quran classes for beginners start exactly here.

Questions and Answers

Arabic Alphabet: Questions Answered

How many letters are in the Arabic alphabet?

The Arabic alphabet has 28 letters, from alif to ya. Some lists count hamza as a 29th letter, because it behaves like a consonant, but most Quran teachers and Qaida books teach 28 letters plus hamza. All the letters are consonants or long vowels, and short vowels are shown with small marks called harakat written above or below the letters.

How long does it take to learn the Arabic alphabet?

Most children learn to recognise and pronounce all 28 Arabic letters in about 4 to 8 weeks with short daily practice and regular classes. Adults often take 2 to 4 weeks. Learning the joining forms and harakat to read simple words takes a few more weeks. A teacher speeds this up by correcting difficult sounds before they become habits.

Is the Quran alphabet the same as the Arabic alphabet?

Yes. The Quran is written in the same 28 letter Arabic alphabet used today. The Quranic script, called the Uthmani script, adds extra marks for recitation, such as signs for stopping, small letters and Tajweed indicators, and some words are spelled in their historic form. A Noorani Qaida prepares students for these marks.

What is the hardest Arabic letter to pronounce?

For English speakers, the hardest letters are usually ع (ayn) and ح (sharp ha) from the throat, and ض (daad), which is unique to Arabic. The heavy letters ص, ط and ظ are also challenging, because they must be kept distinct from their light partners. These letters are best learned by listening to a teacher and being corrected.

Can kids learn the Arabic alphabet online?

Yes. Children learn the Arabic alphabet very well online in short, live classes of 20 to 30 minutes with a patient teacher. The teacher shows each letter on screen, says it clearly and listens to the child repeat it. Four short classes a week work better than one long class, because young children remember through frequent repetition.

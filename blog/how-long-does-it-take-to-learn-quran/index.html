How Long Does It Take to Learn the Quran? Realistic Timelines by Stage

Honest timelines for each stage, from the first letter to fluent Tajweed and full Hifz, for children and adults, with what speeds progress up.

Most beginners can read the Quran slowly within 6 to 12 months of regular classes, and recite fluently with Tajweed within about one to two years. Full memorisation usually takes 2 to 5 years. These are typical ranges, not promises: age, class frequency and daily practice change everything. This guide breaks down realistic timelines for each stage, for children and adults, and explains what speeds progress up or slows it down.

Key takeaways
  • Noorani Qaida: about 3 to 6 months for children, 2 to 4 months for adults.
  • Reading the Quran (Nazra): slow reading in 6 to 12 months from zero, fluent in 1 to 2 years.
  • Tajweed basics: 3 to 6 months once you can read.
  • Hifz: usually 2 to 5 years for the whole Quran.

Timelines by Stage

Every student moves through the same stages. The table shows typical ranges with regular classes and daily practice.

Arabic letters 1 to 2 months 2 to 4 weeks Recognise and say all 28 letters

Nazra (reading) 6 to 12 months 4 to 9 months Read any page slowly and correctly

Why children and adults differ

Young children need more repetition and shorter classes, so the Qaida takes longer. Adults understand rules quickly but may have less daily time. Both reach the goal.

Starting with some knowledge

If you learned as a child and forgot, you may need only weeks to revise the Qaida. A teacher's placement test in the first class saves months.

What Speeds Up Learning

The same student can progress twice as fast with the right habits.

More frequent classes

Four short classes a week beat two, especially for children.

Daily practice

Even 10 minutes a day keeps lessons fresh between classes.

One to one teaching

The student recites for the whole lesson, instead of waiting their turn.

A consistent teacher

One teacher who knows your mistakes saves time every class.

Revision first

Starting each session by revising the last lesson prevents gaps.

Listening

Hearing good recitation daily trains the ear for correct sounds.

Class frequency in practice

A child with four 30 minute classes a week gets about 8 hours of teaching a month. With two classes, it is about 4 hours. Our fees page shows 2 day, 4 day and weekend plans so you can choose.

The right starting book

Skipping the Qaida to "save time" usually costs more time later, fixing letters and rules. See how to learn Quran online for the right order.

What Slows Learning Down

These are the common reasons students stall.

Long gaps

A two week break can undo a month of progress for a young child. If you travel, keep a short online class going or revise at home.

No revision

Students who only learn new lessons forget old ones. This matters most in Hifz, as explained in how to memorise the Quran.

Changing teachers often

Each new teacher spends time learning the student's level and habits. Change only when the match is truly wrong.

Setting Goals That Keep You Going

Long journeys need short milestones.

Monthly milestones

Finish a section of the Qaida, read a full page, recite Al-Fatiha perfectly, memorise one surah. Write each one down.

Celebrate finishing the Qaida

Finishing the Qaida is a big moment, especially for children. Many families mark it with a small celebration before moving to the Quran reading course.

Questions and Answers

How Long to Learn the Quran: Questions Answered

How long does it take to learn to read the Quran?

Most complete beginners can read the Quran slowly and correctly within 6 to 12 months of regular classes, starting with the Noorani Qaida. Adults often reach this point in 4 to 9 months. Reading fluently with good Tajweed usually takes one to two years. Frequent classes, daily practice and a consistent teacher make the biggest difference to speed.

How long does it take to finish the Noorani Qaida?

Children usually finish the Noorani Qaida in 3 to 6 months with three or four short classes a week. Adults often finish in 2 to 4 months because they understand the rules quickly. Very young children, around four, may take longer. Rushing is not helpful, because a strong Qaida makes reading the Quran much easier later.

How long does it take to learn Tajweed?

Once you can read Arabic fluently, the basic Tajweed rules, such as noon sakinah, meem sakinah, ghunnah, qalqalah and madd, usually take 3 to 6 months with regular classes. Applying them confidently across the whole Quran takes about a year of practice. Advanced study for an ijazah takes much longer and requires a qualified teacher.

How long does it take to memorize the Quran?

Most students take 2 to 5 years to memorise the whole Quran. Full-time students may finish in under two years, while part-time students and adults often take longer. At about one new page a day, five days a week, Hifz takes around two and a half years. Strong daily revision is essential to keep what you memorise.

Can I learn the Quran in 3 months?

In three months of regular classes, many adults can finish the Noorani Qaida and begin reading the Quran slowly, and children can learn the letters and much of the Qaida. Fluent reading with Tajweed takes longer. Three months is a great first milestone. Aim for steady daily progress rather than a race, and you will keep what you learn.

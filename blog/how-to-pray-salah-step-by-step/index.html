How to Pray Salah Step by Step: A Simple Guide for Beginners

Every position and every word of the prayer in order, with a rakat chart and tips for teaching children and new Muslims.

Salah, also called namaz, is the five daily prayers every adult Muslim offers to Allah. Each prayer is made of units called rakats, and every rakat follows the same order of standing, bowing, prostrating and sitting. This guide takes you through one complete prayer step by step, with the words to say, a rakat chart and simple tips for teaching children and new Muslims.

Key takeaways
  • Before salah you need wudu, a clean body and place, covered awrah and to face the qiblah.
  • Every rakat has: standing and reciting Al-Fatiha, ruku (bowing), standing up, and two sujood (prostrations).
  • You sit for tashahhud after every two rakats and end the prayer with salam to the right and left.
  • The five prayers have 17 fard rakats a day: Fajr 2, Dhuhr 4, Asr 4, Maghrib 3, Isha 4.

Before You Pray: The Conditions of Salah

A prayer is only valid when certain conditions are met before you begin. These are agreed on by all the main schools of thought.

Purity (taharah)

You need wudu, and if you are in a state that requires a full bath (ghusl), you must take it first. Your body, clothes and the place you pray on should be clean. If you are not sure how to make wudu, read our guide on how to perform wudu step by step, and learn the wudu dua said afterwards.

Covering, direction and time

Men cover at least from the navel to the knees. Women cover the whole body except the face and hands, and many scholars include the feet in what may show. Face the qiblah, the direction of the Kaaba in Makkah. Each prayer has its own time window, so check a reliable local timetable.

Intention (niyyah)

The intention is made in the heart: you know which prayer you are about to pray. You do not need to say it out loud.

The Five Daily Prayers and Their Rakats

Every prayer has a number of fard (obligatory) rakats. The table below shows them. Our full how many rakats chart also covers the sunnah prayers prayed around them.

Prayer Time Fard rakats Recited aloud?
Fajr Dawn until sunrise 2 Yes, both rakats
Dhuhr After midday 4 No, silently
Asr Afternoon 4 No, silently
Maghrib Just after sunset 3 First two rakats aloud
Isha Night 4 First two rakats aloud

Sunnah and Witr

Alongside the fard prayers, the Prophet, peace be upon him, regularly prayed extra sunnah rakats, and Witr is prayed after Isha. We explain these in detail in our guide to sunnah prayers.

Praying in congregation

When praying behind an imam, you follow his movements and do not move before him. Women may pray at home or in the mosque. The steps below are for praying on your own.

How to Pray Salah Step by Step

This is one two-rakat prayer, such as Fajr. Small details, like where the hands are placed, differ slightly between the Hanafi, Shafi'i, Maliki and Hanbali schools, so follow what your family and teacher practise.

Stand and say the takbir. Face the qiblah, raise your hands and say Allahu Akbar (Allah is the Greatest). This begins the prayer.

Fold your hands and recite the opening dua. Place the right hand over the left. Many say Subhanakallahumma wa bihamdika... quietly.

Recite Al-Fatiha. Say A'udhu billahi minash shaytanir rajim, Bismillahir Rahmanir Rahim, then Surah Al-Fatiha. Say Ameen at the end.

Recite another surah. In the first two rakats, add a short surah or some verses, such as Surah Al-Ikhlas.

Ruku (bow). Say Allahu Akbar and bow, hands on knees, back straight. Say Subhana Rabbiyal Azim three times.

Stand up. Rise saying Sami Allahu liman hamidah, then Rabbana wa lakal hamd when fully upright.

Sujood (prostrate). Say Allahu Akbar and go down: forehead, nose, both palms, knees and toes on the ground. Say Subhana Rabbiyal A'la three times.

Sit briefly, then the second sujood. Rise to sitting with Allahu Akbar, pause, then prostrate again the same way. This completes one rakat.

Stand for the second rakat. Repeat steps 3 to 8.

Sit for tashahhud. Recite At-tahiyyatu lillahi..., then the salawat on the Prophet (Durood Ibrahim) and a short dua.

End with salam. Turn your head to the right saying As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah, then to the left saying the same.

Praying three or four rakats

For Maghrib, sit for the first tashahhud after two rakats, then stand for the third. For Dhuhr, Asr and Isha, stand after the first tashahhud for rakats three and four. In these extra rakats of the fard prayer you recite only Al-Fatiha. The final tashahhud, salawat and salam come in the last rakat.

What to recite in Al-Fatiha

Al-Fatiha is recited in every rakat of every prayer. It must be learned correctly, letter by letter, so take time with it. A teacher can listen to your Fatiha and fix any sounds, which is the first thing we do in our online salah classes.

Common Mistakes Beginners Make

Most beginner mistakes are easy to fix once someone points them out.

Rushing the positions

Each position needs a moment of stillness (tuma'ninah). The Prophet, peace be upon him, told a man who rushed his prayer to go back and pray again, because he had not prayed (Sahih al-Bukhari 757). Let your body settle in ruku, standing, sujood and sitting.

Mispronouncing Al-Fatiha

Changing a letter can change a meaning. Common errors are mixing up the heavy and light letters, such as ص and س, or ح and ه. Learning the basics of Tajweed solves most of these.

Forgetting the order

If you forget a step, many scholars teach the prostration of forgetfulness (sujood as-sahw) at the end. Ask a teacher or a local scholar how your school of thought handles it.

Teaching Salah to Children

The Prophet, peace be upon him, said: "Teach your children to pray when they are seven" (Abu Dawud 494, At-Tirmidhi 407). Starting gently and early makes salah a habit, not a fight.

Make it visual and shared

Pray next to your child so they copy you. Put a rakat chart on the wall. Let them choose their own prayer mat. Short surahs from Juz Amma memorisation give them something to recite after Al-Fatiha.

One prayer at a time

Start with one prayer a day, usually Maghrib or Isha when the family is home, then add the others. Praise effort, not perfection. For more ideas, see how to teach Quran to kids at home and our daily duas for kids.

Words they should learn

Children need Al-Fatiha, two or three short surahs, the tasbih of ruku and sujood, tashahhud and Durood Ibrahim. Our online Quran classes for kids teach these alongside reading.

Learning Salah as a New Muslim or Adult

If you are new to Islam, it is fine to start simply while you learn. Scholars allow a new Muslim to say what they can, such as SubhanAllah and Alhamdulillah, while they memorise Al-Fatiha.

A gentle plan for the first month

In week one, learn the movements and pray along with a video or a friend. In week two, memorise Al-Fatiha. In weeks three and four, learn the tashahhud and one short surah. Our Quran classes for new Muslims follow a plan like this with a female teacher.

After the prayer

Many Muslims stay seated after salam for a few minutes of dhikr. Our page on azkar after namaz lists what the Prophet, peace be upon him, said after every prayer.

Questions and Answers

How to Pray Salah: Questions Answered

How do I pray salah step by step as a beginner?

Make wudu, face the qiblah and make the intention in your heart. Say Allahu Akbar, recite Al-Fatiha and a short surah, bow in ruku, stand up, then make two prostrations. That is one rakat. Repeat for the next rakat, sit for tashahhud after every two rakats and in the last one, then end by turning your head right and left with salam.

How many rakats are in each of the five prayers?

The fard rakats are Fajr 2, Dhuhr 4, Asr 4, Maghrib 3 and Isha 4, which makes 17 fard rakats each day. Most Muslims also pray sunnah rakats before or after several of these prayers, and Witr after Isha. The number of sunnah rakats differs slightly between schools of thought, so follow what your family and teacher practise.

What do I say in ruku and sujood?

In ruku say Subhana Rabbiyal Azim, meaning Glory be to my Lord the Most Great, at least three times. In sujood say Subhana Rabbiyal A'la, meaning Glory be to my Lord the Most High, at least three times. When rising from ruku say Sami Allahu liman hamidah, then Rabbana wa lakal hamd once you are standing straight.

At what age should children start praying salah?

The Prophet, peace be upon him, told parents to teach children to pray at seven, as reported by Abu Dawud and At-Tirmidhi. Many parents let younger children join in earlier by copying the movements. By ten, children should be praying regularly. Start with one prayer a day, pray together and praise effort rather than expecting perfection from the start.

Can I pray if I have not memorised Al-Fatiha yet?

Yes. A new Muslim who has not yet memorised Al-Fatiha can pray while learning, saying the words of remembrance they know, such as SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, La ilaha illallah and Allahu Akbar, according to many scholars. Keep working on Al-Fatiha every day, because it is recited in every rakat. A teacher can help you learn it within a few weeks.

Is salah different for women?

The prayer is the same for women and men in its pillars and words. Women cover the whole body except the face and hands, and recite quietly when non-mahram men can hear. Some schools of thought describe small differences in posture for women. Women do not pray during menstruation or post-natal bleeding and do not make up those prayers later.

Noon Sakinah and Tanween Rules: Izhar, Idgham, Iqlab and Ikhfa with Examples

The four rules that apply on almost every page of the Quran, with their letters, clear examples, a one-page chart and tips to remember them.

The rules of noon sakinah and tanween tell you how to pronounce a noon with no vowel (نْ) or a tanween ( ً ٍ ٌ ) depending on the letter that comes after it. There are four rules: Izhar (clear), Idgham (merging), Iqlab (changing) and Ikhfa (hiding). They appear on almost every page of the Quran, so they are the first big rule set every Tajweed student learns. This guide explains each rule with its letters and Quran examples.

Key takeaways
  • Izhar: pronounce the noon clearly before the 6 throat letters ء ه ع ح غ خ.
  • Idgham: merge the noon into the next letter if it is one of ي ر م ل و ن (with or without ghunnah).
  • Iqlab: change the noon into a hidden meem before ب.
  • Ikhfa: hide the noon with ghunnah before the remaining 15 letters.

What Are Noon Sakinah and Tanween?

Before the rules, be sure of the two terms.

Noon sakinah

A noon with a sukoon, or with no vowel at all, such as the noon in مِنْ or أَنْ. It can be in the middle or at the end of a word.

Tanween

The doubled vowel at the end of a noun: fathatain ( ً ), kasratain ( ٍ ) or dammatain ( ٌ ). It is pronounced as an extra "n" sound, like a hidden noon sakinah. That is why the same four rules apply to both.

Why they matter

Getting these rules wrong does not usually change the meaning, but it is one of the clearest marks of recitation without Tajweed. Read what Tajweed is for the difference between clear and hidden mistakes.

The Four Rules at a Glance

This chart is worth printing and keeping next to your mushaf. It is also in our free Tajweed rules PDF, together with all the other main rules.

Rule Meaning Letters What you do Example
Izhar Clear ء ه ع ح غ خ (6) Say the noon clearly, no ghunnah مِنْ عِلْمٍ
Idgham with ghunnah Merging, nasal ي ن م و (4) Merge into the next letter with 2-count ghunnah مَن يَقُولُ
Idgham without ghunnah Merging, no nasal ل ر (2) Merge fully, no ghunnah مِن رَّبِّهِمْ
Iqlab Changing ب (1) Change noon to a hidden meem with ghunnah مِنۢ بَعْدِ
Ikhfa Hiding The other 15 letters Hide the noon with 2-count ghunnah مِن قَبْلِ

A memory tip for Idgham

The six Idgham letters are collected in the word يَرْمَلُونَ (yarmaloon). The four with ghunnah are in يَنْمُو (yanmu).

The 15 Ikhfa letters

ت ث ج د ذ ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ف ق ك. If the letter after noon sakinah is not a throat letter, not in yarmaloon and not ب, it is Ikhfa.

Rule 1: Izhar (Clear Pronunciation)

Izhar means making clear. The noon is pronounced fully, without ghunnah, because the throat letters are far from the place of noon.

The six throat letters

ء ه ع ح غ خ. A common saying to remember them in Arabic is: akhi hadha ilmun hazahu ghayru khasir, where each word begins with a throat letter.

Examples from the Quran

مَنْ آمَنَ (man amana), مِنْ عِلْمٍ (min ilmin), عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ (adhabun alim). The noon or tanween is said clearly before the next letter.

Rule 2: Idgham (Merging)

Idgham means merging: the noon disappears into the next letter, which is then pronounced with a shaddah.

Idgham with ghunnah

Before ي ن م و, the noon merges and a ghunnah of about two counts is held: مَن يَقُولُ (may-yaqulu), مِن نِّعْمَةٍ, خَيْرًا يَرَهُ.

Idgham without ghunnah

Before ل and ر, the noon merges completely with no nasal sound: مِن رَّبِّهِمْ (mir-rabbihim), هُدًى لِّلْمُتَّقِينَ (hudal-lil-muttaqin).

The important exception

Idgham only happens across two words. When noon sakinah and a yarmaloon letter are in the same word, Izhar applies. There are four such words in the Quran: الدُّنْيَا, بُنْيَانٌ, قِنْوَانٌ and صِنْوَانٌ.

Rule 3: Iqlab (Changing)

Iqlab means changing. When noon sakinah or tanween comes before ب, the noon changes into a meem, said with a light closing of the lips and a two-count ghunnah.

How to spot it

Most mushafs print a small meem (ۢ) above the noon or tanween to show Iqlab: مِنۢ بَعْدِ (mim-ba'di), سَمِيعٌۢ بَصِيرٌ, أَنۢبِئْهُم.

A common mistake

Do not press the lips hard together as for a full meem. Bring them together lightly while holding the ghunnah, then move to the ب.

Rule 4: Ikhfa (Hiding)

Ikhfa means hiding. The noon is not fully pronounced and not fully merged. It is hidden in a nasal sound held for about two counts, while the tongue prepares for the next letter.

Heavy and light Ikhfa

Before heavy letters (ص ض ط ظ ق), the ghunnah sounds deeper, as in مِن قَبْلِ. Before light letters, it sounds lighter, as in أَنتُمْ and مِن شَرِّ.

Practise with Surah Al-Falaq

Surah Al-Falaq contains Ikhfa several times in مِن شَرِّ. Recite it slowly, holding each ghunnah for two counts. Colour-coded mushafs mark Ikhfa clearly; see our guide to the colour-coded Tajweed Quran.

How to Master These Rules

Knowing the chart is not the same as reciting correctly. Practise in this order.

Learn the letters of each rule by heart, using yarmaloon and the throat letters.

Spot the rules on one page of Juz Amma with a pencil.

Recite slowly that page, applying each rule.

Recite to a teacher who checks your ghunnah length and your Ikhfa.

Move to meem sakinah, which has three similar rules, then qalqalah and madd.

Getting the sound right

Ikhfa and Iqlab are about sound, which is learned by ear. Our online Tajweed classes focus on exactly this, and our Tajweed rules for beginners gives you the whole system on one page. Correct letter articulation comes first, explained in makharij al-huroof.

Move on to meem sakinah

Meem sakinah has three rules that mirror these: Ikhfa shafawi before ب, Idgham shafawi before م and Izhar shafawi before all other letters. Learn them next, while noon sakinah is still fresh.

Questions and Answers

Noon Sakinah Rules: Questions Answered

What are the 4 rules of noon sakinah and tanween?

The four rules are Izhar, Idgham, Iqlab and Ikhfa. Izhar means saying the noon clearly before the six throat letters. Idgham means merging it into ي ر م ل و ن, with ghunnah for four of them. Iqlab means changing it to a hidden meem before ب. Ikhfa means hiding it with ghunnah before the remaining fifteen letters.

What are the letters of Ikhfa?

The fifteen letters of Ikhfa are ت ث ج د ذ ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ف ق ك. When noon sakinah or tanween comes before any of them, the noon is hidden with a ghunnah of about two counts. An easy way to remember is that Ikhfa covers every letter not used for Izhar, Idgham or Iqlab.

What is the difference between Idgham and Ikhfa?

In Idgham, the noon sakinah merges completely into the next letter, which then takes a shaddah, as in man-yaqulu becoming may-yaqulu. In Ikhfa, the noon is neither fully pronounced nor fully merged. It is hidden in a nasal sound while the tongue prepares for the next letter. Idgham uses six letters and Ikhfa uses fifteen.

How long is the ghunnah in Ikhfa and Idgham?

The ghunnah in Ikhfa, Iqlab and Idgham with ghunnah is held for about two counts, the length of two short vowels. Teachers often measure a count by the time it takes to fold or open one finger at a steady pace. The ghunnah should be smooth and even. A teacher will help you find the right length by ear.

Why is it Izhar in the word dunya?

Idgham only happens when the noon sakinah is at the end of one word and the Idgham letter starts the next word. In الدُّنْيَا the noon and ya are in the same word, so merging would make the word unclear. The same applies to بنيان, قنوان and صنوان, the four words where this rule, called Izhar mutlaq, appears.

How to Prepare Kids for Ramadan: 15 Practical Ideas for Parents

Practice fasts by age, a good deeds chart, child-sized Quran goals, duas to learn and fifteen simple ideas to make Ramadan the best month of the year for kids.

Children remember how Ramadan felt long before they understand its rules. A month of family iftars, small fasts, Quran goals and good deeds can plant a love for Ramadan that lasts a lifetime. With Ramadan 2027 expected to begin around 8 February, the next week is the perfect time to prepare. This guide covers practice fasting by age, Quran and dua goals for kids, a good deeds chart and fifteen simple ideas.

Key takeaways
  • Fasting becomes obligatory at puberty. Before that, practice fasts build the habit gently.
  • Set child-sized goals: a surah a week, a dua a day, a good deed a day.
  • Make the house feel different: lights, a countdown, a special iftar role for each child.
  • Focus on why we fast, not just the hunger: gratitude, patience and closeness to Allah.

Explaining Ramadan to Children

Start with simple, positive explanations a child can repeat.

What Ramadan is

Ramadan is the month when the Quran was first revealed. Muslims fast from dawn to sunset, pray more, read more Quran and give more charity. Explain that it is a gift from Allah, a month when good deeds grow.

Why we fast

Allah tells us fasting helps us gain taqwa, being aware of Allah (Quran 2:183). For children, say: fasting helps us remember Allah, be patient, and feel thankful for food that others do not have.

The night better than a thousand months

Tell older children about Laylatul Qadr, a night in the last ten nights that is better than a thousand months. See our guide to the last ten nights of Ramadan.

Practice Fasting by Age

Children are not required to fast until puberty, but the companions trained their children with practice fasts. Ar-Rubayyi bint Mu'awwidh said they made their children fast and gave them toys made of wool to distract them until iftar (Sahih al-Bukhari 1960).

Age Suggested practice Tip
Under 6 No fasting. Join suhoor or iftar, small "no snack" times Let them feel part of it
6 to 8 Half-day fasts, such as until Dhuhr, on weekends Praise loudly at the end
8 to 10 Some full fasts, especially on weekends Plenty of water and food at suhoor
10 to puberty Most days if healthy, building to the full month Watch energy at school

Keep it healthy

Make suhoor filling with slow foods, water and fruit. Stop a child's practice fast if they feel unwell. Practice is about love for the habit, not endurance.

Talk to school

If an older child fasts on school days, let teachers know, especially for sports days.

Quran and Dua Goals for Kids

Ramadan is the month of the Quran, for children too. Our Ramadan Quran plan has goals for every level.

Qaida stage

Finish a set of Qaida lessons or the whole Qaida by Eid.

Reading stage

Read Juz Amma during the month, a few pages a day with a parent.

Memorising

One short surah a week, four by Eid. See short surahs for kids.

Duas

Learn the iftar dua and the Laylatul Qadr dua from our Ramadan duas.

The iftar dua

Listen together

Play recitation in the car and at home. Hearing Juz Amma daily makes memorising easier.

15 Ideas to Make Ramadan Special for Kids

Pick a few that suit your family. You do not need all fifteen.

A countdown calendar to Ramadan and then to Eid, with a small deed in each box.

A good deeds chart with stickers for prayers, Quran pages and kindness.

Decorate together with lights and a moon and stars banner.

An iftar job for each child: dates, water, setting the table.

A charity jar for coins, given away before Eid.

Cook for neighbours and let children deliver a plate.

Story time with a Prophet story each night. Try our Islamic stories for kids.

A Ramadan journal to draw or write one thing they are thankful for each day.

A "dua jar" where children write duas to make before iftar.

Pray Maghrib together right after breaking the fast.

Short Taraweeh at home with the surahs children know.

A kindness challenge, such as one kind act a day for a sibling.

Thank you cards for teachers, grandparents or the imam.

A Quran khatam party for the family, even if everyone read different parts.

Eid gifts made by children for family members.

Keep screens in check

Long fasting days can turn into screen days. Replace some screen time with Islamic audiobooks, crafts or helping prepare iftar.

Make the last ten nights special

Let older children stay up a little on odd nights for a short prayer, dua and a treat.

Teaching Good Character in Ramadan

Fasting is more than hunger. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said if someone insults a fasting person, they should say "I am fasting" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1904).

Patience and kind words

Make "I am fasting" a family phrase that means "I choose not to argue". Praise children who stay calm.

Gratitude and generosity

Talk about children who do not have enough food. Give charity together. Our online duas course and online Quran classes for kids build these values alongside recitation.

Questions and Answers

Kids and Ramadan: Questions Answered

At what age should kids start fasting in Ramadan?

Fasting becomes obligatory at puberty. Before that, many families start practice fasts around age 6 to 8 with half days, then build up to some full fasts by 9 or 10, if the child is healthy. The companions trained their children to fast and distracted them with toys until iftar, as reported in Sahih al-Bukhari. Keep it gentle and positive.

How can I explain Ramadan to a young child?

Use simple words: Ramadan is a special month when Allah first sent the Quran, and Muslims fast from morning until sunset to remember Allah, be patient and be thankful. We also pray more, read more Quran and help people. Let your child join suhoor or iftar and give them a small job, so they feel part of it.

What duas should kids learn for Ramadan?

Children can learn the iftar dua, Dhahaba dhama'u wabtallatil uruq wa thabatal ajru in sha Allah, and the dua for Laylatul Qadr, Allahumma innaka afuwwun tuhibbul afwa fa'fu anni. Older children can learn the dua on seeing the new moon. Teach one at a time and say it together at the right moment each day.

How can kids read Quran in Ramadan?

Set a goal that suits their level: finishing part of the Noorani Qaida, reading Juz Amma during the month, or memorising one short surah each week. Read together for 10 to 15 minutes after Fajr or before iftar, use a sticker chart and celebrate at Eid. Keeping regular Quran classes in Ramadan also helps a lot.

Should my child keep Quran classes in Ramadan?

Yes, if the timing works. Ramadan is one of the best times to learn, because motivation is high. Shift classes away from the tired late afternoon, for example to after school or after Taraweeh, and keep them short. Our teachers adjust class times in Ramadan so children can continue without stress.

Ramadan Quran Plan: How to Finish the Quran in Ramadan 2027

Simple reading schedules to complete the Quran in Ramadan, with plans for fluent readers, beginners, children and busy mothers, and tips to start before Ramadan.

Ramadan is the month of the Quran. To finish the whole Quran in 30 days, read one juz a day, which is about 20 pages: just 4 pages after each of the five daily prayers. Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin around 8 February, depending on the moon sighting, so now is the time to plan. This guide gives simple reading schedules for one or more khatams, plans for beginners, children and busy mothers, and tips to start strong.

Key takeaways
  • One khatam in 30 days = 1 juz a day = about 20 pages = 4 pages after each prayer.
  • If you are a beginner, set a realistic goal such as one surah, one juz or the Qaida, not a full khatam.
  • Start before Ramadan: read a few pages daily from now so your pace is ready.
  • Quality over quantity: recite with Tajweed and reflect on some meaning every day.

Why Ramadan Is the Month of the Quran

Allah connects Ramadan directly with the Quran:

The Prophet's practice

Jibreel would meet the Prophet, peace be upon him, every night of Ramadan and study the Quran with him (Sahih al-Bukhari 6). In his final year, they reviewed it twice (Sahih al-Bukhari 4998).

Multiplied reward

Every letter of the Quran already earns ten good deeds (At-Tirmidhi 2910). In the blessed month, and especially on Laylatul Qadr, the opportunity is even greater. See more benefits of reciting the Quran.

Plans to Finish the Quran in Ramadan

The standard Madani mushaf has 604 pages and 30 juz. Choose the plan that fits your reading speed.

Goal Per day After each of 5 prayers Time a day (fluent reader)
1 khatam in 30 days 1 juz (about 20 pages) 4 pages About 40 to 60 minutes
1 khatam in 20 days 1.5 juz (about 30 pages) 6 pages About 60 to 90 minutes
2 khatams in 30 days 2 juz (about 40 pages) 8 pages About 1.5 to 2 hours
Half the Quran in 30 days Half a juz (about 10 pages) 2 pages About 20 to 30 minutes

The 4 pages after every prayer method

This is the most popular method because it spreads reading across the day. Four pages takes about 8 to 12 minutes for a fluent reader. Done after each fard prayer, it completes one juz daily without a big block of time.

Taraweeh and your plan

If your mosque completes the Quran in Taraweeh, listening adds to your connection with the Quran, but your own recitation is separate. Keep your daily pages too.

Catching up

Missed a day? Add 1 page after each prayer for the next 4 days. Small catch-ups work better than one exhausting session.

Plans for Beginners and Slow Readers

A full khatam is not the only goal. For a beginner, steady progress is the real success.

Still learning the Qaida

Aim to finish a set of Qaida lessons and memorise one new short surah.

Reading slowly

Read Juz Amma (the 30th juz) through the month, a little each day.

Reading comfortably

Aim for 5 to 10 pages a day, about one third of the Quran.

Improving Tajweed

Choose quality: 2 to 3 pages a day with careful Tajweed and a teacher.

Count small wins

A beginner who finishes the Qaida or reads Juz Amma properly in Ramadan has achieved something great. Track pages or lessons on a chart and celebrate at Eid.

Plans for Busy Mothers and Children

Ramadan for a mother means suhoor, iftar, children and often work. The plan must be gentle.

For mothers

Read in small pieces: 2 pages after Fajr while the house is quiet, 2 after Dhuhr, a few while children nap. Listen to recitation while cooking. If you are not praying for some days, many scholars allow reading from memory, listening and dhikr. Our Quran classes for women fit around these routines.

For children

Set a child-sized goal: one short surah memorised per week, a Qaida milestone or a juz read with a parent. A Ramadan chart with stickers works wonders. See how to prepare kids for Ramadan.

Getting Ready Before Ramadan Starts

The best Ramadan plans start weeks before the month.

Start now with 2 to 4 pages a day, to build your reading speed.

Pick your goal from the tables above and write it down.

Mark your mushaf with a bookmark for each prayer's portion, or use a checklist.

Fix your Tajweed on Al-Fatiha and the surahs you recite in salah.

Learn the Ramadan duas, including the iftar dua and the dua for Laylatul Qadr, from our Ramadan duas page.

Plan around your energy

Many people read best after Fajr and after Taraweeh, and struggle in the late afternoon. Put your biggest portion in your strongest time.

Reflect, do not only race

Choose one verse a day to read in translation and think about. A khatam with reflection changes the heart. Our Tafseer for beginners guide helps you start.

Questions and Answers

Ramadan Quran Plan: Questions Answered

How can I finish the Quran in Ramadan?

Read one juz a day, which is about 20 pages, to finish the Quran in 30 days. The easiest way is to read 4 pages after each of the five daily prayers, which takes about 8 to 12 minutes each time for a fluent reader. If you miss a day, add one extra page after each prayer for the next few days.

How many pages should I read a day to finish the Quran in Ramadan?

The standard Madani mushaf has 604 pages, so you need about 20 pages a day to finish in 30 days, or about 21 pages a day if Ramadan has 29 days. That is 4 pages after each fard prayer, plus a page extra on some days. For two khatams, read about 40 pages a day.

Is it necessary to finish the Quran in Ramadan?

Finishing the whole Quran in Ramadan is not obligatory, but it is a beloved practice following the example of the Prophet, peace be upon him, who reviewed the Quran with Jibreel every Ramadan. If you cannot read fluently yet, set a smaller goal, such as one juz or the Qaida, and focus on improving your recitation.

When does Ramadan 2027 start?

Ramadan 2027 is expected to begin around 8 February 2027, depending on the sighting of the moon in your country. The exact date is announced by local moon sighting committees and mosques a day or two before. Eid al-Fitr is expected around 9 or 10 March 2027. Check with your local mosque for confirmed dates.

Can I read the Quran in Ramadan if I cannot read Arabic well?

Yes. Read what you can, slowly and carefully, and you will be rewarded, as the one who struggles with the Quran gets a double reward. You can also listen to recitation with translation. Ramadan is a great time to start Quran classes, so you can read more confidently by next Ramadan.

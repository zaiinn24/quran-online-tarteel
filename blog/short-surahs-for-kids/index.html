Short Surahs for Kids: The First 14 Surahs to Memorize, in Order

The fourteen short surahs most children learn first, in a sensible order, with verse counts, one-line meanings and a weekly plan that works.

The first surahs children memorise are usually Al-Fatiha and the short surahs at the end of the Quran, in Juz Amma. They are short, rhythmic and used in every salah, so children see the benefit straight away. This guide lists the 14 short surahs most children learn first, in a sensible order, with verse counts and one-line meanings, plus a weekly plan and tips that help children memorise happily.

Key takeaways
  • Start with Al-Fatiha, then the 3 Quls, then move backwards through Juz Amma.
  • One short surah a week is a good pace for young children.
  • Learn by listening first, then by repeating line by line, then by reciting to a teacher.
  • Use the surahs in salah and at bedtime, which is the best revision.

The First 14 Short Surahs, in Order

This order starts with the most needed and the shortest, then builds up.

Order Surah Number Verses Meaning in one line
1 Al-Fatiha 1 7 Praise to Allah and asking Him for guidance, recited in every rakat
2 Al-Ikhlas 112 4 Allah is One, He has no parent, child or equal
3 Al-Falaq 113 5 Seeking Allah's protection from evil around us
4 An-Nas 114 6 Seeking Allah's protection from whispers inside us
5 Al-Kawthar 108 3 Allah gave the Prophet abundant good, so pray and sacrifice
6 Al-Asr 103 3 People are at a loss except those who believe, do good and advise each other
7 An-Nasr 110 3 When Allah's help comes, praise Him and seek forgiveness
8 Al-Kafirun 109 6 I worship Allah alone; you have your religion and I have mine
9 Al-Masad 111 5 The fate of Abu Lahab, who opposed the Prophet
10 Quraysh 106 4 Thank Allah who fed and protected the Quraysh
11 Al-Ma'un 107 7 Do not neglect prayer or refuse small kindnesses
12 Al-Fil 105 5 Allah protected the Kaaba from the army of the elephant
13 Al-Humazah 104 9 A warning against mocking people and loving wealth
14 At-Takathur 102 8 Competing for more distracts us until the grave

Why Al-Fatiha comes first

Salah is not complete without Al-Fatiha, so it is the most important surah for every child to learn correctly, with Tajweed.

Why the Quls come early

The Prophet, peace be upon him, recited Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas every night before sleep (Sahih al-Bukhari 5017). Al-Ikhlas equals a third of the Quran in reward (Sahih al-Bukhari 5013). See them in full on our page for Ayatul Kursi and the 4 Quls.

A Weekly Memorisation Plan

For children aged about 4 to 9, one short surah a week is a good pace. Longer surahs can take two weeks.

Monday: listen to the new surah five times with your child, then explain its meaning simply.

Tuesday: learn the first half, line by line, repeating each line 5 to 10 times.

Wednesday: learn the second half the same way.

Thursday: join the whole surah and recite it three times.

Friday: recite to the teacher or a parent, with Tajweed corrections.

Weekend: use it in salah and at bedtime, and revise last week's surah.

Revise old surahs every day

Each day, recite one or two older surahs too. Children forget quickly without revision, as explained in how to memorise the Quran.

Keep a surah chart

Put a chart on the wall with all 14 surahs. Add a star when a surah is memorised and another star when it is perfect with Tajweed.

Tips for Parents

Small habits keep memorisation joyful.

Listen in the car

Play the surah of the week on every journey.

Use it in salah

Let your child recite their new surah aloud when you pray together.

Explain one lesson

Al-Asr teaches not to waste time, Al-Ma'un teaches kindness.

Praise effort

Celebrate trying, not only finishing.

Short sessions

10 to 15 minutes is enough for young children.

Teacher for Tajweed

Memorising with mistakes is hard to undo later.

Link to stories

Many short surahs have stories. Al-Fil tells of the year of the elephant, Al-Masad of Abu Lahab. Children remember surahs better when they know the story. Try our Islamic stories for kids.

Keep it gentle

Never use memorisation as a punishment. Read how to teach Quran to kids at home for more ideas.

After the First 14 Surahs

Once these are strong, children continue backwards through Juz Amma.

Completing Juz Amma

The 30th juz has 37 surahs. Most children complete it in one to two years. Our Juz Amma memorisation course follows this path.

Next steps

After Juz Amma, many families add Surah Al-Mulk, then decide whether to start full Hifz. Read what Hifz is and the best age to start and see our online Hifz program.

Questions and Answers

Short Surahs for Kids: Questions Answered

Which surah should kids memorize first?

Most children memorise Surah Al-Fatiha first, because it is recited in every rakat of salah. After that, they usually learn the three Quls, Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas, followed by other short surahs from the end of Juz Amma such as Al-Kawthar, Al-Asr and An-Nasr. Starting with the shortest surahs builds confidence quickly.

What is the shortest surah in the Quran?

The shortest surah in the Quran is Surah Al-Kawthar, the 108th surah, with three short verses. Surah Al-Asr and Surah An-Nasr also have three verses each, but their verses are longer. These short surahs are ideal for young children to memorise early and to recite in their salah.

How many short surahs should a child memorize?

There is no fixed number, but every child should memorise Al-Fatiha and at least a few short surahs for salah. A good goal is the 14 short surahs listed here, then the whole of Juz Amma, which has 37 surahs. At one short surah a week, most children learn the first 14 in three to four months.

How can I help my child memorize surahs faster?

Play the surah repeatedly so your child hears it many times, then teach it line by line, repeating each line 5 to 10 times. Join the lines, revise daily and let your child recite the surah in salah and at bedtime. Short daily sessions and a patient teacher who corrects Tajweed make the biggest difference.

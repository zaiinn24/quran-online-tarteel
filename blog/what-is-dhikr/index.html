What Is Dhikr? Meaning, Types, Benefits and a Daily Dhikr List

What dhikr means, why the Quran calls it the greatest act, the best words to say and a simple daily dhikr routine for busy families.

Dhikr means remembering Allah: with the tongue, in the heart and through actions. The Arabic word ذكر literally means remembrance or mention. In daily life, dhikr usually refers to short phrases such as SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah and Allahu Akbar, repeated with attention. This guide explains the meaning of dhikr in Islam, its types and benefits, the best words to say and a simple daily dhikr list.

Key takeaways
  • Dhikr meaning: remembrance of Allah, by heart, tongue and action.
  • Allah says: "Remember Me; I will remember you" (Quran 2:152).
  • The best dhikr is La ilaha illallah (At-Tirmidhi 3383), and the Quran itself is the greatest dhikr.
  • Easy daily routine: morning and evening azkar, dhikr after each salah and before sleep.

What Does Dhikr Mean in Islam?

Dhikr is any act of remembering Allah. Reciting the Quran, making dua, praising Allah and even thinking about His creation with gratitude are all forms of dhikr.

Dhikr in the Quran

The Quran repeatedly commands remembrance:

Dhikr and dua

Dhikr praises Allah. Dua asks Him for something. Many duas begin with dhikr, which is why the two are often mentioned together. Read our full guide on what dua is and how to make it.

Azkar, adhkar and dhikr

Adhkar (also spelled azkar) is the plural of dhikr. You can download all the morning and evening adhkar as a free morning and evening adhkar PDF. It usually refers to set words for set times, such as the morning and evening azkar or the azkar after namaz.

Types of Dhikr

Scholars describe dhikr in three overlapping types.

Dhikr of the tongue

Saying words of praise aloud or quietly, such as SubhanAllah or Astaghfirullah.

Dhikr of the heart

Being aware of Allah, reflecting on His names and His blessings.

Dhikr in action

Obeying Allah, such as praying salah, which Allah calls remembrance (Quran 20:14).

Fixed and free dhikr

Fixed dhikr has set words for set times, such as after salah or before sleep. Free dhikr is anything you say at any time, for example while driving or cooking.

Dhikr out loud or silently

Most personal dhikr is said quietly. Some adhkar, such as the takbir on Eid, are said aloud. Avoid disturbing others in the mosque.

Benefits of Dhikr from Quran and Hadith

The rewards of dhikr are large compared with how easy it is.

Protection

Several morning and evening adhkar are protection for the day or night, such as reciting Ayatul Kursi and the 4 Quls.

Peace for anxious hearts

People struggling with worry often find relief in regular dhikr and the Prophet's duas for distress. See our duas for anxiety.

The Best Dhikr to Say Every Day

You do not need a long list. These short phrases come from authentic hadith and can be said anywhere.

The Quran is the greatest dhikr

Allah calls the Quran itself adh-Dhikr (Quran 15:9). A few verses a day count as remembrance. See the benefits of reciting the Quran.

Salawat on the Prophet

Sending blessings on the Prophet, peace be upon him, is also dhikr. Whoever sends one blessing on him, Allah sends ten on them (Sahih Muslim 408).

A Simple Daily Dhikr Routine

Here is a routine a busy parent or student can keep. Start with one part and add the rest over time.

After Fajr: the morning azkar, about 10 minutes.

After each salah: Astaghfirullah 3 times, then SubhanAllah 33, Alhamdulillah 33, Allahu Akbar 33 or 34, and Ayatul Kursi. Full text in azkar after namaz.

During the day: SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi 100 times, spread across commutes and chores.

After Asr or Maghrib: the evening azkar.

Before sleep: the duas before sleeping, Ayatul Kursi and the 3 Quls.

Counting dhikr

The Prophet, peace be upon him, counted tasbih on the fingers of his right hand and said the fingers will be asked and made to speak (Abu Dawud 1501). Many people also use a tasbih (prayer beads).

Teaching dhikr to children

Say dhikr aloud together in the car or at bedtime. Children love counting 33 on their fingers. Our daily duas for kids and the online duas course build these habits gently.

Questions and Answers

Dhikr in Islam: Questions Answered

What is the meaning of dhikr in Islam?

Dhikr means remembrance of Allah. It includes saying words of praise such as SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah and Allahu Akbar, reciting the Quran, making dua and keeping Allah in mind through the day. The Quran says hearts find rest in the remembrance of Allah, in Surah Ar-Ra'd verse 28, and Allah promises to remember those who remember Him.

What is the best dhikr?

The Prophet, peace be upon him, said the best dhikr is La ilaha illallah, as narrated by At-Tirmidhi. He also praised SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi, SubhanAllahil Azim as two words light on the tongue but heavy on the scale. Reciting the Quran is the greatest form of remembrance overall, and Astaghfirullah is a dhikr he said more than 70 times daily.

What dhikr should I say after salah?

After salam, say Astaghfirullah three times, then Allahumma antas salam wa minkas salam, tabarakta ya dhal jalali wal ikram. Then say SubhanAllah 33 times, Alhamdulillah 33 times and Allahu Akbar 33 times, completing 100 with La ilaha illallahu wahdahu la sharika lah. Recite Ayatul Kursi too. These are reported in Sahih Muslim and other collections.

Can I do dhikr without wudu?

Yes. Dhikr such as SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, istighfar and salawat can be said without wudu, and even by women during menstruation, according to scholars. The Prophet, peace be upon him, remembered Allah in all his states, as Aishah reported in Sahih Muslim. Wudu is recommended but not required for dhikr, unlike salah and touching the mushaf.

How many times should I do dhikr a day?

There is no fixed total. Some adhkar have set numbers, such as 33 of each tasbih after salah or SubhanAllahi wa bihamdihi 100 times a day. Beyond that, the Quran praises those who remember Allah much. Start with the morning and evening adhkar and dhikr after each prayer, then add more as it becomes a habit.

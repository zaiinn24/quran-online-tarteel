How It Works

How Do Online Quran Classes Work in Oman?

Online Quran classes in Oman are live video lessons in which one student learns to read, recite or memorise the Quran with one teacher over the internet. The student sees the Quran page on screen, repeats after the teacher and is corrected straight away. At Quran Online Tarteel, every teacher is female and every class is one-to-one.

No traffic, no long drives

There is no drive across Muscat to a Quran centre and no waiting outside in the heat. Your child logs in from home at the booked time and the lesson starts.

Who can join from Oman

Boys and girls under 18 and adult women can join, from first letters to fluent recitation. We do not enrol adult men, which keeps every class comfortable for families and women.

Questions and Answers

Online Quran Classes in Oman: Frequently Asked Questions

How much do online Quran classes cost in Oman?

Monthly fees in Oman for Noorani Qaida, Quran reading (Nazra) and Tajweed are OMR 15.5 for 2 days a week, OMR 23.5 for 4 days a week and OMR 23.5 for weekend classes (Saturday and Sunday), with 25–30 minute classes. Every new student starts with 2 free classes, so you pay nothing until you decide to continue. See the full fees table for every course.

Do you teach families in Muscat, Salalah and Sohar?

Yes. We teach families anywhere in Oman, including Muscat, Seeb, Salalah, Sohar, Nizwa, Sur and Ibri. All classes are online and booked in Oman time, so it makes no difference where you live, even in smaller towns far from a Quran centre. You only need a phone, tablet or laptop and a steady internet connection.

Are there online Quran classes for women in Oman?

Yes. Women of any age in Oman can learn to read the Quran, improve Tajweed, memorise surahs or study translation and Tafseer with a female teacher. Classes are private and one-to-one, and many mothers book lessons while the children are at school. We do not enrol adult men. See our online Quran classes for women.

What class times are available in Oman?

Classes run after school, in the evening and at weekends, and on weekday mornings where time zones allow, all booked in Gulf Standard Time (GST, UTC+4). Pakistan is only one hour ahead of Oman, so almost every slot from morning to evening is easy to book. Oman does not change its clocks, so your class time stays the same all year. Our weekend plan runs on Saturday and Sunday.

Do your teachers teach in Arabic, Urdu and English?

Yes. Our teachers explain in English, Urdu or Arabic, whichever your child understands best. Many Pakistani and Indian families in Oman choose Urdu, Arab families often prefer Arabic, and children at international schools usually prefer English. The Quran itself is always read in Arabic. Tell us your preference when you book and we will match the right teacher.

How do I pay for online Quran classes from Oman?

Families abroad usually pay by bank transfer or Western Union, or a relative in Pakistan pays for them by JazzCash, Easypaisa or bank transfer. The fee is paid monthly, in advance, after your 2 free classes, and we share the payment details on WhatsApp. We do not take card or PayPal payments at the moment. The monthly fees in Omani rials are in the fees table on this page.

Can my child keep learning if we move back to Pakistan?

Yes. Many families in Oman move for work, and online classes go wherever you go. If you return to Pakistan or move to another country, your child keeps the same female teacher, the same lesson plan and the same progress. Because Pakistan is only one hour ahead of Oman, the class time usually changes very little after the move.

Are online Quran classes safe for children in Oman?

Yes. Every teacher is female, every class is one-to-one, and we only teach children under 18 and adult women. The class link and timing always go to the parent, never directly to the child, and parents are welcome to sit in on any lesson. We never record classes. Read our FAQ and safeguarding page for the full rules.

Are classes held during Ramadan?

Yes. In Ramadan the class timings change, and we agree new times with you before the month begins, around suhoor, school and iftar. Classes are off on Eid and on Pakistan's public holidays. See our Ramadan Quran plan for ideas to make the month count.

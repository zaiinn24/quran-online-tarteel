Tajweed is the set of rules for reciting the Quran the way it was revealed to the Prophet, peace be upon him: every letter from its correct place, with its correct quality, length and nasal sound. The word comes from the Arabic root *jawwada*, to make something excellent. This guide explains the meaning of Tajweed, why it matters, the main rules in one table and how to learn it properly.

:::key
- **Tajweed meaning:** giving every letter its right, from its correct point of articulation and with its correct qualities.
- Scholars divide mistakes into **clear mistakes** (which change a letter or meaning) and **hidden mistakes** (which spoil beauty).
- The core topics are **makharij, sifaat, noon and meem sakinah, ghunnah, qalqalah and madd**.
- Tajweed is learned by **listening and being corrected**, not from books alone.
:::

## What Does Tajweed Mean?

Linguistically, tajweed (تجويد) means to improve or to do something well. Technically, scholars of recitation define it as pronouncing every letter from its point of articulation (makhraj) and giving it its rights and dues.

### The rights and dues of a letter

The "right" (haqq) of a letter is its permanent qualities, such as whether it is heavy or light, whispered or voiced. The "due" (mustahaq) is what it takes on in certain positions, such as merging into the next letter or being lengthened. Tajweed teaches both.

### Tajweed and tarteel

Allah says in Surah Al-Muzzammil:

:::quote
ayah: 73:4
en: And recite the Quran with measured recitation (tartil).
:::

Many scholars explained *tartil* as reciting slowly and clearly, giving letters their due and knowing where to pause. Tajweed is the science that makes tarteel possible. It is also where our academy's name, Quran Online Tarteel, comes from.

## Why Tajweed Matters

Tajweed is not decoration. Without it, the meaning of a verse can change.

### Clear mistakes (lahn jali)

A clear mistake changes a letter, a vowel or a word. For example, reading the heavy ص as a light س, or a short vowel as long. Scholars agree that avoiding clear mistakes is an obligation for anyone reciting the Quran, because they can change the meaning.

### Hidden mistakes (lahn khafi)

A hidden mistake does not change the meaning but spoils the recitation, such as cutting a ghunnah short or not holding a madd for its full count. These are noticed by people who know Tajweed. Students work on them once clear mistakes are fixed.

### Reward for the struggling reciter

No one should give up because Tajweed feels hard. The Prophet, peace be upon him, said that the one who is skilled in the Quran will be with the noble, righteous scribes, and the one who recites it and stumbles, finding it difficult, will have a double reward (Sahih al-Bukhari 4937, Sahih Muslim 798).

### Start with makharij

Every Tajweed teacher starts with makharij, because if a letter comes from the wrong place, no rule can fix it. Read our full guide to [makharij al-huroof](/blog/makharij-al-huroof/).

### Then the rules of noon and meem

The rules of [noon sakinah and tanween](/blog/noon-sakinah-and-tanween-rules/) appear on almost every page of the Quran, so they come next. After that, students learn [qalqalah](/blog/qalqalah-in-tajweed/) and [madd](/blog/madd-in-tajweed/).

## How to Learn Tajweed

Tajweed was passed from teacher to student, all the way back to the Prophet, peace be upon him. That chain is still the best way to learn.

Q: What is the simple meaning of Tajweed?
A: Tajweed means reciting the Quran correctly and beautifully, giving every Arabic letter its proper sound, place of articulation, length and nasal quality. The word comes from an Arabic root meaning to make something good or excellent. In practice, Tajweed is a set of rules, such as ghunnah, qalqalah and madd, that make your recitation match the way the Quran was revealed and taught.
Q: Is learning Tajweed compulsory?
A: Scholars of recitation say avoiding clear mistakes, which change letters or meanings, is obligatory for anyone reciting the Quran. Learning the detailed theory of every rule is a communal duty, not required of each person. In practice, every Muslim should learn enough Tajweed to recite Al-Fatiha and the surahs used in salah without changing any letter or meaning.
Q: How long does it take to learn Tajweed?
A: Most students learn the basic rules of Tajweed in 3 to 6 months of regular classes, and apply them confidently across the Quran within about a year. The time depends on whether you can already read fluently, how many classes you take each week and how much you practise. Children who learn Tajweed with the Qaida often master it naturally while reading.
Q: Can I learn Tajweed online?
A: Yes. Tajweed is taught by listening and correcting, which works very well on a live video call. The teacher hears your recitation, points to the exact letter that needs fixing and models the correct sound. Choose one to one classes so the teacher listens to you for the whole lesson, rather than a recorded course with no feedback.
Q: What is the difference between Tajweed and Tarteel?
A: Tarteel is the measured, clear way of reciting the Quran that Allah commands in Surah Al-Muzzammil, verse 4. Tajweed is the science of rules that makes that recitation correct, such as makharij, ghunnah and madd. Tarteel also describes a slow pace of recitation, while Tajweed applies at every pace, slow or fast.
Q: What should I learn first in Tajweed?
A: Start with makharij, the points of articulation, so every letter comes from its correct place. Then learn the qualities of letters, followed by the rules of noon sakinah and tanween, meem sakinah, ghunnah, qalqalah and madd. A teacher will usually correct your Al-Fatiha first, because it is recited in every rakat of every prayer.
:::

A good female Quran teacher combines three things: sound knowledge of Tajweed, the patience to teach, and the reliability to turn up every week. Many sisters and parents of daughters, and of young sons too, prefer a female teacher for comfort and modesty. This checklist shows what qualifications to look for, what to ask, how to judge a trial class and which safeguarding rules every family should expect.

:::key
- Look for **formal Tajweed study** (ideally an ijazah), **teaching experience** and **clear communication in your language**.
- Judge the teacher in a **live trial class**, not by a profile or a video.
- For children, expect **clear safeguarding**: parent nearby, no private chats, classes in a shared space.
- A good academy lets you **change teacher easily** if the match is not right.
:::

## Why Many Families Choose a Female Quran Teacher

Choosing a female teacher is a personal decision, and there are good reasons behind it.

### Comfort and modesty for sisters

Adult women often feel more relaxed reciting, asking questions and correcting mistakes with another woman. They can also learn at home without worrying about dress or camera settings. Our [Quran classes for women](/online-quran-classes-for-women/) are built around this.

### Gentle teaching for young children

Many parents find female teachers especially warm and patient with young children, both girls and boys. At our academy, every teacher is female and we teach children (boys and girls under 18) and women only. Meet them on our [female Quran teachers](/female-quran-teachers/) page.

### Mothers learning alongside children

When the teacher is female, mothers can sit in, learn the same lesson and help at home. Some families book back-to-back classes for mother and child.

## Qualifications to Look For

A friendly manner is not enough. These are the qualifications that matter.

| Qualification | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Ijazah or sanad | Certified by a teacher with a chain back to the Prophet, peace be upon him | Strong sign of accurate recitation |
| Tajweed certificate | Completed a recognised Tajweed course | Knows the rules and can explain them |
| Hafizah (for Hifz) | Has memorised the whole Quran | Needed to teach and test Hifz properly |
| Teaching experience | Years teaching children or adults | Knows how to handle different learners |
| Your language | Explains in English, Urdu or Arabic | Faster understanding, fewer mix-ups |

### Not every teacher needs every qualification

A Noorani Qaida teacher for a five year old needs excellent pronunciation and patience more than an advanced ijazah. A Hifz teacher must be a hafizah. Match the teacher to the course.

### Ask, do not assume

It is perfectly polite to ask a teacher where and with whom she studied. Good teachers are happy to answer.

## Questions to Ask Before You Book

Use these questions with any academy or tutor.

:::steps
1. **Where did the teacher study Tajweed, and does she hold an ijazah?**
2. **How many years has she taught, and which ages?**
3. **Which language will she explain in?**
4. **Are classes one to one and live?** How long is each class?
5. **How will progress be reported** to me or to parents?
6. **What happens if we want to change teacher** or reschedule a class?
7. **What are the safeguarding rules** for children's classes?
8. **Is there a free trial,** and what does it include?
:::

### Questions specific to children

Ask how the teacher keeps young children focused, how she handles a shy or restless child, and whether she uses visuals on screen. See [how to teach Quran to kids at home](/blog/how-to-teach-quran-to-kids-at-home/) for what works at home.

### Questions specific to adult sisters

Ask whether the teacher can work with irregular schedules, whether she can start where you are rather than from page one, and whether she can include meaning or Tafseer later.

## How to Judge a Trial Class

The trial is your best tool. Pay attention to these signs.

:::cards
- **She listens more than she talks** | The student should recite for most of the class.
- **Corrections are precise** | She names the letter or rule, models it and has the student repeat.
- **Encouraging tone** | Mistakes are corrected kindly, effort is praised.
- **She checks your level** | She does not just start from page one without asking.
- **Clear next steps** | You leave knowing what to practise before the next class.
- **On time, good connection** | Small things that show reliability over months.
:::

### After the trial

Ask yourself, or your child: did you feel comfortable, did you learn something specific, and do you want another class? If two of these are "no", try another teacher.

## Safeguarding Rules Every Family Should Expect

Online classes for children must be safe. These rules should be standard.

### For children's classes

Classes take place in a shared family space, not a closed bedroom. A parent is at home and can see the screen. No private messaging, social media contact or phone calls between the teacher and the child. Communication goes through parents. Our full rules are on the [FAQ and safeguarding](/faq/) page.

### For adult sisters

You control your camera. Many sisters keep video on for the teacher to see the page, or share only the screen. Your details should never be shared.

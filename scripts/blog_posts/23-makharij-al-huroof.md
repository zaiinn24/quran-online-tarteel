Makharij al-huroof (مخارج الحروف) are the points of articulation: the exact places in the mouth and throat where each Arabic letter is produced. Most scholars of Tajweed, following Imam Ibn al-Jazari, count 17 points grouped in 5 main areas. If a letter comes from the wrong place, it becomes a different letter, so makharij are the very first thing a Tajweed student learns. This guide explains each area, the letters it produces and how to practise.

:::key
- **5 main areas:** the empty space of the mouth and throat, the throat, the tongue, the lips and the nasal passage.
- **17 points** in total, following the widely taught view of Ibn al-Jazari.
- The **tongue** produces the most letters, from 10 different points.
- To find a letter's makhraj, put a **sukoon** on it with a hamza before it: أَبْ, أَتْ, أَقْ.
:::

## What Are Makharij and Why Do They Matter?

*Makhraj* means "exit" or "place of coming out". In Tajweed it is the point where the sound of a letter is formed.

### A wrong makhraj changes the word

Saying ح from the chest instead of the throat turns it into ه. In some words this changes the meaning entirely. Correct makharij are part of avoiding the clear mistakes explained in [what Tajweed is](/blog/what-is-tajweed/).

### How to test a letter's makhraj

Say the letter with a sukoon after a hamza with fatha: أَبْ, أَعْ, أَقْ. Where the sound stops is its makhraj. Teachers use this in every first Tajweed class.

## The Five Areas of Articulation

The 17 points are grouped into five main areas.

| Area | Arabic name | Number of points | Letters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Empty space of mouth and throat | Al-Jawf | 1 | The three long vowels: ا, و and ي as madd letters |
| Throat | Al-Halq | 3 | ء ه, ع ح, غ خ |
| Tongue | Al-Lisan | 10 | ق ك ج ش ي ض ل ن ر ط د ت ص س ز ظ ذ ث |
| Lips | Ash-Shafatan | 2 | ف, and ب م و |
| Nasal passage | Al-Khayshum | 1 | Ghunnah of noon and meem |

### Al-Jawf: the empty space

The long vowels (madd letters) have no fixed point. Their sound flows through the empty space of the mouth and throat and ends when the breath ends. This is why they can be lengthened, as in [madd rules](/blog/madd-in-tajweed/).

### Al-Khayshum: the nasal passage

Ghunnah, the nasal sound in noon and meem, comes from the nose. Pinch your nose while saying a ghunnah and the sound stops. That is how you know it is working.

## The Throat Letters

The throat has three points, each producing two letters.

:::cards 3
- **Deepest part of the throat** | ء and ه. Hamza is a sharp stop, ha is a soft breath.
- **Middle of the throat** | ع and ح. The hardest for English speakers. Ayn is voiced, ha is a breathy whisper.
- **Top of the throat** | غ and خ. Close to the mouth. Ghayn is voiced like a gargle, kha is rough and whispered.
:::

### Common throat mistakes

Saying ع as a plain vowel, ح as ه, and خ as a k. Practise each pair with a teacher who can hear the difference.

### Why they are called throat letters

These six letters are also the letters of Izhar in [noon sakinah and tanween rules](/blog/noon-sakinah-and-tanween-rules/), because they come from far away from noon.

## The Tongue Letters

The tongue produces 18 letters from 10 points. Here they are from the back of the tongue to the tip.

| Point | Letters | How it is made |
|---|---|---|
| Back of the tongue with the soft palate | ق | Deepest part of the tongue, heavy |
| Back of the tongue, a little forward | ك | Slightly forward of qaf, light |
| Middle of the tongue with the palate | ج ش ي | Middle of the tongue rises |
| Side edge of the tongue with the upper molars | ض | Unique to Arabic, one or both sides |
| Front edges of the tongue | ل | Edges of the tongue to the gums |
| Tip of the tongue, under lam | ن | Slightly forward of lam |
| Tip of the tongue, near noon | ر | Tip and a little of the top of the tongue |
| Tip of the tongue with the roots of the upper front teeth | ط د ت | Ta heavy, dal and ta light |
| Tip of the tongue near the lower front teeth | ص س ز | Whistling letters |
| Tip of the tongue with the edges of the upper front teeth | ظ ذ ث | Tongue tip slightly out |

### Qaf and kaf

Both come from the back of the tongue, but qaf is deeper and heavy, kaf is further forward and light. Mixing them changes words, for example قلب (heart) and كلب (dog).

### The unique daad

ض is so special that Arabic is called "the language of daad". It is often mispronounced as a heavy dal or as ظ. Learn it carefully with a teacher.

## The Lip Letters

The lips have two points.

### Lower lip with upper teeth: ف

The inside of the lower lip touches the edges of the upper front teeth, as in English "f".

### Both lips: ب م و

Ba closes the lips firmly, meem closes them with a nasal sound, and waw rounds them without closing. These lip letters connect to the meem sakinah rules.

## How to Practise Makharij

Makharij are physical. You learn them by feeling and hearing, not by reading alone.

:::steps
1. **Learn one area at a time,** starting with the throat letters.
2. **Use the sukoon test:** أَعْ, أَحْ, أَغْ, until each sound is distinct.
3. **Use a mirror** for lip and tongue-tip letters.
4. **Record yourself** and compare with a trusted reciter.
5. **Recite to a teacher** who corrects you the moment a letter drifts.
:::

:::faq
Q: What are makharij al-huroof?
A: Makharij al-huroof are the points of articulation of the Arabic letters, meaning the exact places in the throat, tongue, lips, nasal passage and the open space of the mouth where each letter's sound is produced. Learning them ensures every letter is pronounced correctly and distinctly, which is the foundation of Tajweed and of correct Quran recitation.
Q: How many makharij are there?
A: Most Tajweed scholars, following Imam Ibn al-Jazari, count 17 makharij grouped in 5 main areas: the empty space of the mouth and throat, the throat, the tongue, the lips and the nasal passage. Some scholars count 16 or 14 by merging certain points, but 17 is the most widely taught view in Quran schools today.
Q: Which letters come from the throat?
A: Six letters come from the throat, in three pairs. Hamza and ha come from the deepest part, ayn and the sharp ha from the middle, and ghayn and kha from the top, closest to the mouth. These six are also the letters of Izhar, so noon sakinah and tanween are pronounced clearly before them.
Q: How do I find the makhraj of a letter?
A: Put a sukoon on the letter and say it after a hamza with fatha, for example أَبْ, أَعْ or أَقْ. The place where the sound stops and is formed is the letter's makhraj. Teachers use this test to check each letter. A mirror helps for lip and tongue-tip letters, and a teacher's ear helps for throat letters.
Q: Why is daad called a unique letter?
A: The letter ض, daad, is produced from the side edge of the tongue against the upper molars, a sound not found in most languages. Arabic is even called the language of daad. It is often confused with a heavy dal or with ظ, so students should learn it carefully by listening to a qualified teacher.
:::

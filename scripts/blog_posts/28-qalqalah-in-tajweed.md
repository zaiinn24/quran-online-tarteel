Qalqalah is a slight echo or bounce in the sound of five Arabic letters when they carry a sukoon. The letters are ق ط ب ج د, remembered together as **قُطْبُ جَدٍّ** (qutbu jad). Without qalqalah, these letters would almost disappear when they have no vowel. With it, they stay clear. This guide explains the five letters, the levels of qalqalah, how to pronounce it correctly and examples from the surahs you already know.

:::key
- **Qalqalah letters:** ق ط ب ج د, remembered as **qutbu jad**.
- It happens when the letter has a **sukoon**, whether written or caused by stopping.
- **Minor (sughra):** in the middle of reading, a light bounce. **Major (kubra):** when stopping, a stronger bounce.
- The echo must **not** become a vowel: no "a", "i" or "u" sound after the letter.
:::

## What Is Qalqalah?

The word qalqalah means to shake or disturb. In Tajweed, it describes the small vibration that releases these letters.

### Why these five letters?

The five qalqalah letters share two qualities: they are strong (shiddah), so the sound is fully blocked, and voiced (jahr), so the breath is held. With a sukoon, the sound would be trapped, so a small bounce releases it.

### Where it fits in Tajweed

Qalqalah is one of the qualities of letters (sifaat), studied after [makharij al-huroof](/blog/makharij-al-huroof/) and alongside the [noon sakinah rules](/blog/noon-sakinah-and-tanween-rules/). See the full picture in [what Tajweed is](/blog/what-is-tajweed/).

## The Levels of Qalqalah

Most teachers explain qalqalah in two main levels, and some add a third.

| Level | When it happens | Strength | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sughra (minor) | Letter has a written sukoon in the middle of reading | Light | <span class="ar">يَجْعَلُونَ</span>, <span class="ar">قَدْ أَفْلَحَ</span> |
| Kubra (major) | Letter at the end of a word where you stop | Stronger | <span class="ar">أَحَدْ</span>, <span class="ar">ٱلْفَلَقْ</span> |
| Akbar (greatest, in some books) | Stopping on a qalqalah letter with shaddah | Strongest | <span class="ar">ٱلْحَقّْ</span> at a stop |

### Minor qalqalah

The letter has a sukoon inside a word or at the end of a word you continue past. The bounce is short and light: <span class="ar">ٱقْرَأْ</span>, <span class="ar">أَطْعَمَهُم</span>, <span class="ar">يَجْعَلُونَ</span>.

### Major qalqalah

When you stop on a word ending in a qalqalah letter, the stop creates a sukoon and the bounce is clearer. Surah Al-Ikhlas is full of them: <span class="ar">أَحَدْ</span>, <span class="ar">ٱلصَّمَدْ</span>, <span class="ar">يُولَدْ</span>.

## How to Pronounce Qalqalah Correctly

The sound is subtle. These points help.

:::steps
1. **Make the letter from its correct makhraj,** fully closing the point of articulation.
2. **Release it with a small bounce,** as the closure opens.
3. **Do not add a vowel.** No "qa", "di" or "bu" sound after the letter.
4. **Keep it short.** The bounce is quick, not a long echo.
5. **Make it stronger when stopping** at the end of a verse.
:::

### The direction of the sound

Teachers differ on whether the bounce follows the vowel before it or is always slightly towards fatha. Follow your teacher, who will model the sound for you.

### Heavy and light qalqalah letters

ق and ط are heavy letters, so their bounce sounds fuller. ب, ج and د are light. Keep each letter's quality while bouncing.

## Common Qalqalah Mistakes

These are the mistakes teachers hear most often.

### Adding a vowel

Turning أَحَدْ into "ahada" is the most common error. The sound should bounce but not become a vowel.

### Skipping qalqalah

Swallowing the letter, so أَحَدْ sounds like "aha". The dal must be heard.

### Applying it to the wrong letters

Only the five letters of qutbu jad have qalqalah. Letters like ك and ت are released with a breath, not a bounce. :::faq
Q: What are the qalqalah letters?
A: The five qalqalah letters are ق, ط, ب, ج and د. They are collected in the Arabic phrase qutbu jad, قُطْبُ جَدٍّ. When any of these letters carries a sukoon, either written or caused by stopping, it is pronounced with a slight echo or bounce so that its sound is heard clearly rather than swallowed.
Q: What is the difference between qalqalah sughra and kubra?
A: Qalqalah sughra, or minor, happens when a qalqalah letter has a written sukoon in the middle of reading, and the bounce is light, as in يَجْعَلُونَ. Qalqalah kubra, or major, happens when you stop on a word ending in a qalqalah letter, and the bounce is stronger, as in أَحَدْ at the end of Surah Al-Ikhlas.
Q: How do you pronounce qalqalah?
A: Pronounce the letter from its correct makhraj, closing the point of articulation completely, then release it with a small, quick bounce without adding any vowel sound. The bounce should be light in the middle of reading and a little stronger when stopping. Listening to a teacher and imitating is the most reliable way to learn the exact sound.
Q: Is there qalqalah on a letter with a vowel?
A: No. Qalqalah only applies when the letter has no vowel, meaning a written sukoon or a sukoon caused by stopping at the end of a word. When ق, ط, ب, ج or د carries a fatha, kasra or damma, it is pronounced normally with that vowel and there is no bounce.
:::

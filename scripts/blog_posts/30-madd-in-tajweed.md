Madd means lengthening. In Tajweed, it is the stretching of a vowel sound beyond its normal length, measured in counts (harakat). The basic madd lasts 2 counts; other types stretch to 4, 5 or 6 counts depending on what comes after the madd letter. Getting madd right gives recitation its flowing, measured sound. This guide explains the madd letters and the main types of madd, with counts and examples from the surahs you already know. The counts follow the recitation of Hafs from Asim, the most widely used today.

:::key
- **Madd letters:** ا after fatha, و sakinah after damma, ي sakinah after kasra.
- **Natural madd (tabi'i):** 2 counts. The base of all madd.
- **Hamza or sukoon after a madd letter** creates longer madd: 4, 5 or 6 counts.
- A wavy line (~) above a letter in most mushafs signals a **longer madd**.
:::

## The Madd Letters

Three letters can be madd letters, when they follow a matching vowel.

| Madd letter | Condition | Example | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alif (ا) | After a fatha | <span class="ar">قَالَ</span> | "qaa-la" |
| Waw (و) with sukoon | After a damma | <span class="ar">يَقُولُ</span> | "ya-qoo-lu" |
| Ya (ي) with sukoon | After a kasra | <span class="ar">قِيلَ</span> | "qee-la" |

### What is a count?

A count (harakah) is the length of one short vowel. Teachers often measure counts by folding or unfolding a finger at a steady pace. Consistency matters more than exact timing.

### Why madd matters

Shortening a long vowel, or lengthening a short one, can change the meaning of a word. This is why madd mistakes can be clear mistakes, as explained in [what Tajweed is](/blog/what-is-tajweed/).

## Natural Madd (Madd Tabi'i)

The natural madd is a madd letter not followed by a hamza or a sukoon. It is held for exactly 2 counts.

### Examples

<span class="ar">قَالَ</span>, <span class="ar">نُوحِيهَا</span>, <span class="ar">يَقُولُ</span>. Almost every line of the Quran has several.

### The most common mistake

Rushing the natural madd to one count, or stretching it to three or four. Keep it a steady two.

## Madd Caused by Hamza

When a hamza follows a madd letter, the madd becomes longer.

### Madd muttasil (connected): 4 or 5 counts

The madd letter and hamza are in the same word: <span class="ar">جَآءَ</span>, <span class="ar">ٱلسَّمَآءِ</span>, <span class="ar">سُوٓءَ</span>. It is called *wajib* (necessary), because it must be lengthened. In Hafs via Ash-Shatibiyyah, it is 4 or 5 counts.

### Madd munfasil (separated): 2, 4 or 5 counts

The madd letter ends one word and the hamza starts the next: <span class="ar">يَٰٓأَيُّهَا</span>, <span class="ar">بِمَآ أُنزِلَ</span>, <span class="ar">قُوٓا۟ أَنفُسَكُمْ</span>. It is called *ja'iz* (permissible). Many teachers teach 4 or 5 counts for Hafs; keep it consistent through your recitation.

### Madd badal: 2 counts

A hamza comes before the madd letter: <span class="ar">ءَامَنُوا۟</span>, <span class="ar">إِيمَٰنًا</span>, <span class="ar">أُوتُوا۟</span>. In Hafs it is held for 2 counts, like natural madd.

## Madd Caused by Sukoon

When a sukoon follows a madd letter, the madd can become the longest of all.

| Type | When | Counts | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Madd lazim (necessary) | Permanent sukoon or shaddah after a madd letter | 6 | <span class="ar">ٱلضَّآلِّينَ</span>, <span class="ar">ٱلْحَآقَّةُ</span> |
| Madd lazim harfi | Disjointed letters at the start of some surahs | 6 for most letters | <span class="ar">الٓمٓ</span> (Alif Laaam Meeem) |
| Madd 'aarid lis-sukoon | Stopping on a word whose last letter follows a madd letter | 2, 4 or 6 | <span class="ar">ٱلرَّحِيمْ</span>, <span class="ar">نَسْتَعِينْ</span> |
| Madd leen | Stopping after و or ي with sukoon after a fatha | 2, 4 or 6 | <span class="ar">خَوْفْ</span>, <span class="ar">ٱلْبَيْتْ</span> |

### Madd lazim in Al-Fatiha

The last word of Al-Fatiha, <span class="ar">وَلَا ٱلضَّآلِّينَ</span>, has a madd lazim of 6 counts, because the alif is followed by a lam with shaddah. This is one of the first long madds children learn.

### Madd 'aarid at the end of verses

When you stop at the end of a verse such as <span class="ar">ٱلرَّحِيمِ</span>, the last vowel becomes a sukoon, so the madd before it may be 2, 4 or 6 counts. Choose one length and keep it the same across the recitation.

## All Types at a Glance

| Type | Counts (Hafs) |
|---|---|
| Natural (tabi'i) | 2 |
| Badal | 2 |
| Muttasil (connected) | 4 or 5 |
| Munfasil (separated) | 4 or 5 (2 is also narrated) |
| 'Aarid lis-sukoon | 2, 4 or 6 |
| Leen | 2, 4 or 6 |
| Lazim | 6 |

### Spotting madd in the mushaf

Most mushafs place a wavy line (~) over a madd letter when the madd is longer than 2 counts. In a [colour-coded Tajweed Quran](/blog/colour-coded-tajweed-quran/), madd lengths are shown in different shades.

### Choose one length and keep it

Where a madd allows more than one length, such as madd 'aarid, choose one and use it consistently in the same recitation. Mixing lengths is a common hidden mistake.

## How to Practise Madd

Madd is all about consistent length. These steps help.

:::steps
1. **Practise natural madd** with Al-Fatiha until every 2 count is even.
2. **Find the wavy lines** on one page of Juz Amma and mark muttasil and munfasil.
3. **Recite slowly with a finger count** for each long madd.
4. **Record yourself** and compare with a trusted reciter.
5. **Recite to a teacher** who checks your consistency, as in our [online Tajweed classes](/online-tajweed-classes/).
:::

### Practise with Al-Fatiha daily

Al-Fatiha contains natural madd, madd 'aarid at the verse ends and madd lazim in its last word. Reciting it carefully every day trains your counts.

:::faq
Q: What is madd in Tajweed?
A: Madd means lengthening a vowel sound beyond its normal length when reciting the Quran. It happens on the three madd letters: alif after a fatha, waw with sukoon after a damma and ya with sukoon after a kasra. The basic madd lasts 2 counts, and it becomes longer, up to 6 counts, when followed by a hamza or a sukoon.
Q: How many types of madd are there?
A: Madd has two main groups: the natural madd of 2 counts, and the secondary madds caused by a hamza or a sukoon. The common secondary types are madd muttasil, madd munfasil, madd badal, madd lazim, madd aarid lis-sukoon and madd leen. Some books list more subdivisions, but these cover what most students need for recitation.
Q: What is the difference between madd muttasil and munfasil?
A: In madd muttasil, the madd letter and the hamza after it are in the same word, as in جَآءَ, and it must be lengthened 4 or 5 counts in Hafs. In madd munfasil, the madd letter ends one word and the hamza begins the next, as in يَٰٓأَيُّهَا, and it is permissible to lengthen it 2, 4 or 5 counts.
Q: How long is madd lazim?
A: Madd lazim is always 6 counts. It happens when a madd letter is followed by a permanent sukoon or a shaddah in the same word, as in ٱلضَّآلِّينَ at the end of Surah Al-Fatiha, or in the disjointed letters at the start of some surahs, such as Laaam and Meeem in الٓمٓ. It is the longest madd in Tajweed.
Q: What does the wavy line above a letter mean in the Quran?
A: The wavy line, called the madd sign, shows that the madd on that letter is longer than the natural 2 counts. It usually appears on madd muttasil, madd munfasil and madd lazim. The exact length depends on the type of madd, so students learn the rules to know whether to hold it for 4, 5 or 6 counts.
:::

Surah Al-Kahf (The Cave) is the 18th surah of the Quran, with 110 verses. The Prophet, peace be upon him, taught that whoever reads it on Friday will have a light between the two Fridays, and that its first ten verses protect from the trial of the Dajjal. Its four stories each address a great trial: faith, wealth, knowledge and power. This guide explains its benefits, the best time to read it, its stories and lessons, and tips to make it a weekly habit.

:::key
- Reading Al-Kahf on Friday brings **light between the two Fridays** (Al-Hakim, graded sahih by Al-Albani).
- Memorising its **first ten verses** protects from the **Dajjal** (Sahih Muslim 809).
- Friday begins at **Maghrib on Thursday** and ends at **Maghrib on Friday**.
- Its four stories cover four trials: **faith, wealth, knowledge and power**.
:::

## The Benefits of Surah Al-Kahf

The virtues of reading Al-Kahf come from hadith graded authentic by scholars.

### Light between two Fridays

:::quote
en: Whoever reads Surah Al-Kahf on the day of Jumu'ah, a light will shine for him between the two Fridays.
ref: Al-Hakim, Al-Bayhaqi; graded sahih by Al-Albani (Sahih al-Jami 6470)
:::

### Protection from the Dajjal

"Whoever memorises ten verses from the beginning of Surah Al-Kahf will be protected from the Dajjal" (Sahih Muslim 809). In another narration in Sahih Muslim, the Prophet, peace be upon him, told the believers to recite its opening verses against him.

### A weekly reminder

Reading the same surah every week keeps its lessons fresh. Many families read it together after Jumu'ah or on Thursday night.

## When to Read Surah Al-Kahf

In the Islamic calendar, a day begins at sunset.

### The Friday window

You can read it from Maghrib on Thursday evening until Maghrib on Friday. Many scholars mention this full window, so it is fine to read it on Thursday night.

### Splitting it up

At about 11 to 12 pages in the standard Madani mushaf, Al-Kahf takes roughly 25 to 40 minutes. You can split it: half after Fajr, half after Jumu'ah or Asr.

### Listening or reading

If you cannot read Arabic yet, listen to it with a translation while you learn. Our [online Quran classes for beginners](/learn-quran-online-for-beginners/) help you read it yourself.

## The Four Stories of Surah Al-Kahf

Scholars often link the four stories to four trials of life, which the Dajjal will also use.

| Story | Verses | Trial | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| The People of the Cave | 9 to 26 | Trial of faith | Hold on to faith; Allah protects those who turn to Him |
| The Owner of Two Gardens | 32 to 44 | Trial of wealth | Wealth belongs to Allah; be grateful, not arrogant |
| Musa and Al-Khidr | 60 to 82 | Trial of knowledge | Be humble; Allah's wisdom is beyond what we see |
| Dhul-Qarnayn | 83 to 98 | Trial of power | Use power justly and credit Allah for every success |

### The People of the Cave

Young believers fled persecution and sheltered in a cave. Their dua before sleeping is a dua we can make too:

:::quote
dua: For mercy and guidance
:::

Allah made them sleep for many years and woke them as a sign of His power.

### The Owner of Two Gardens

A man proud of his gardens forgot Allah. His wealth was destroyed in a night, and his believing friend reminded him to say *ma sha Allah, la quwwata illa billah* (18:39).

### Musa and Al-Khidr

Musa, peace be upon him, travelled to learn from Al-Khidr, who did three things that looked wrong but had hidden wisdom. Lesson: humility in seeking knowledge.

### Dhul-Qarnayn

A just ruler who travelled east and west, helped a people by building a barrier and said: "This is a mercy from my Lord" (18:98). Children love this story; see our [Islamic stories for kids](/blog/islamic-stories-for-kids/).

## How to Make Al-Kahf a Weekly Habit

A few small steps make Friday reading stick.

:::steps
1. **Set a fixed time:** Thursday after Isha or Friday after Fajr.
2. **Use a bookmark** that stays in Al-Kahf.
3. **Split it in two** if a single sitting is too long.
4. **Read one story's meaning each week,** cycling through all four in a month.
5. **Memorise the first ten verses,** then keep reciting them weekly.
:::

:::faq
Q: Why do Muslims read Surah Al-Kahf on Friday?
A: Muslims read Surah Al-Kahf on Friday because the Prophet, peace be upon him, said that whoever reads it on the day of Jumu'ah will have a light shining for him between the two Fridays. This hadith is reported by Al-Hakim and Al-Bayhaqi and graded sahih by Al-Albani. Its stories also protect against major trials, including the Dajjal.
Q: When should I read Surah Al-Kahf on Friday?
A: You can read Surah Al-Kahf any time from Maghrib on Thursday evening until Maghrib on Friday, because the Islamic day begins at sunset. Many people read it after Fajr on Friday, after Jumu'ah prayer or on Thursday night. You can also split it into two or three sittings during that time.
Q: What are the four stories in Surah Al-Kahf?
A: Surah Al-Kahf contains four stories: the People of the Cave, young believers protected by Allah; the owner of two gardens, who was proud of his wealth; Musa and Al-Khidr, a journey of humble learning; and Dhul-Qarnayn, a just and powerful ruler. Scholars link them to the trials of faith, wealth, knowledge and power.
Q: What is the benefit of the first 10 verses of Surah Al-Kahf?
A: The Prophet, peace be upon him, said whoever memorises the first ten verses of Surah Al-Kahf will be protected from the Dajjal, as narrated in Sahih Muslim. Many Muslims memorise these verses and recite them regularly. They are a realistic memorisation goal for children and adults, taking a week or two for most learners.
Q: How long does it take to read Surah Al-Kahf?
A: Surah Al-Kahf has 110 verses, about 11 to 12 pages in the standard Madani mushaf. A fluent reader usually takes about 25 to 40 minutes, depending on pace and Tajweed. Beginners may take longer, so splitting it between Thursday night and Friday works well. Listening to a recitation takes around 25 to 30 minutes.
:::
